## 1. What Godseye IS (in plain terms)

**Godseye is an AI agent that manages WordPress websites for you — through Telegram, in plain English.**

You type what you want done ("publish a post about our new sale," "fix the broken image on the homepage," "check why my store isn't showing orders"). Godseye does it. No plugins to fiddle with, no dashboard to learn, no code to write.

It's the "one AI employee for your website" — it can create and edit posts, manage products and store orders (WooCommerce), upload media, respond to comments, run security checks, schedule recurring tasks, and show you analytics. All from a chat.

**Fast formula (memorize this):**
> "Your WordPress site, managed by AI. Chat. It works. No code."

## 5. Brand voice & tone (HARD rules)

- **Confident, calm, futuristic.** Think high-end AI tool, not hypey scam.
- **No jargon.** "Manage your WordPress site" — never "utilize the content management system."
- **Speak to the reader's site, not to tech.** "Your site. Your store. One chat."
- **Show the magic, don't claim it.** Demo > adjectives.
- **FOMO is allowed but must be genuine** — low founder pricing that won't last. No fake urgency.

**Gold standard tone example:**
> "Every WordPress task used to mean paying someone. Now it's one message. 'Write and publish our weekly post.' Done. That's Godseye."

## 10. Visual / brand standards

- **Colors:** near-black background `#0A0A0A`, gold accent `#C4A484`, off-white text `#F2F2F2`.
- **Fonts:** Headings = Georgia serif (light). Body = Inter sans. Labels = monospace.
- **Mood:** dark, premium, "all-seeing eye," futuristic. The Godseye mark is a gold eye on a dark roundel.
- **Keep it clean:** lots of negative space, single gold focal accent, no clutter.
- **Avoid:** bright multi-colors, comic sans, garish gradients, "AI slop" rainbow vibes.

## 11. Calls to action (CTA rules)

- **Every asset ends with one clear action:** tap the button / message the bot / visit the site.
- **Messenger framing:** "Message the bot on Telegram" (NOT "text" or "DM").
