## 3 · ICON / LOGO NOTE + THUMBNAIL

**Logo (the PH icon — 240×240 square):**
- Dark, near-black background `#0A0A0A`, gold `#C4A484` mark, Georgia serif wordmark cut back to the capital **G** inside a thin gold ring (the "all-seeing eye" as a subtle aperture inside the G counter). Keep it flat, one accent color, high contrast on dark thumbnails.
- If you already have a `logo/godseye-*.png` asset, upload it as-is and just confirm it renders crisp at 240px (no thin strokes that read as noise at small size). Minimum safe export: 600×600 PNG (transparent or dark bg), no rounded-square crop baked in — ProductHunt applies its own mask.
- On-brand check: gold on `#0A0A0A`, Georgia serif only for the wordmark; the mark should read as *caretaker + guardian*, not a robot.

**Thumbnail recommendation (the wide cover image):**
- Use a framed mock of the real Telegram conversation — the dark chat surface (`#121212` cards, `#F2F2F2` text, gold accent for the agent's messages) with one crisp exchange visible: e.g. *"Update the footer and publish the new post."* → *"Done. Footer live, post scheduled for 9am."*
- Aspect 16:9 (ProductHunt cover). Keep >70% of the frame dark with the text a few clean lines — it must read instantly on a phone-sized feed and survive as a tiny thumbnail.
- Do NOT use a generic robot/neural-network stock image. The moat is *human-likable*; the cover should show a person-like conversation, not electronica.

### 5 recycling replies — string them up as comments appear

**Objection 1 — price ("$9 is cheap, $99 is a lot …")**
```
Fair. The way to think about it: one freelance dev retainer is usually $200–$500 a month. GodsEye sits in front of the everyday work — updates, content, security, uptime — so you hire it instead of a developer for the busywork. Why not kick the tyres at $9 for the first month and judge it on the receipts?
```

**Objection 2 — trust / brand-new product ("Not sure I trust a startup with my site")**
```
Completely reasonable, and I'd say the same. That's why the agent never ships unapproved changes on new connections without asking, why there's a real 30-day usage receipt you can read, and why the templates and agent work are open-source enough to eyeball. Start on a staging or low-risk site, watch how it asks permission, then let it loose.
```

**Objection 3 — "will it break my site?"**
```
Bad news would advertise itself fast, so fair to worry. The agent proposes changes and waits for your OK before anything touches your live site — updates, edits, publishes all follow your approval gate. It logs everything, and you can revert or tune what it's allowed to touch. Safest first move: connect one site and watch it ask before it acts.
```

**Objection 4 — "how is it better than just hiring a dev?"**
```
A dev is great at the hard, one-off stuff — GodsEye is built for the endless everyday. It answers in seconds, never bills a surprise hour, learns your voice, and works 24/7 without a ticket queue. For a solopreneur, a dev on retainer for routine updates is overkill; this handles the volume and you keep a dev for the deep builds.
```

**Objection 5 — security ("what access do you have / is Telegram safe?")**
```
Access is scoped and revocable: you connect a dedicated application password per site, the agent acts only on what you approve, and you can cut access from chat at any time. Telegram sessions are end-to-end on your side, and the agent reports what it did so actions are auditable. Least-privilege first, always.
```
