How much does maintaining a WordPress site really cost?

Field notes from the Godseye team · 5 min read Guide

Ask a small business owner what their website costs and they'll name the design fee — the one they paid once. The real number, the one nobody budgets for, is the monthly upkeep: the developer visit when something breaks, the plugin subscriptions that pile up, the agency retainer for "peace of mind." That number is usually more than they think.

The per-task cost is the trap

WordPress doesn't fail often — but when it does, you pay a premium because you need it fixed now. A quick look at what a small site actually spends:

  • Developer visits — $50–$150 per task. Update an image, fix a broken page, troubleshoot a plugin conflict. Each visit is a new invoice.
  • Plugin subscriptions — $5–$30 each, several of them, annualized without a second thought.
  • Emergency fixes — the site is down, customers are calling, and the rate doubles.
  • Your own time — the hours spent googling error messages, which most owners quietly don't count at all.

Add it up and a "free" WordPress site can run you a few hundred dollars a year in upkeep — spread across dozens of separate, surprise invoices.

Why it always feels like so much

Because you're paying for repeated small jobs, not one product. Each one carries a minimum callout fee, a wait, and a new bill. That's not a website problem — that's a "per-task" pricing problem. The moment you stop paying per task and pay a flat thing instead, the math changes completely.

What a flat plan looks like

Imagine the routine work — publishing a post, updating a product image, answering a comment, running a security check — handled in one chat conversation, no invoice per job. That's the model that stops the bleed:

「 update the sale banner on our homepage and publish this week's post 」

Done. No developer visit, no ticket queue, no separate charge. The upkeep that used to come as ten surprise invoices now comes as part of one plan.

Check before you sign — the real questions

  • Is it per task, or flat? Flat beats per-task for anything you do more than once a month.
  • Do I stay in control? You should approve everything before it touches your live site.
  • Is it one place? If your site, your shop, and your team are all on separate apps, you're paying the same attention tax.

Stop paying per task

Your WordPress site doesn't have to run on surprise invoices. Routine management belongs in the plan — handled in a chat you already have open. That's the whole pitch: one flat thing, your site managed, no per-job billing.
