The 7 tasks to hand an AI agent first

Field notes from the Godseye team · 7 min read Guide

You don't hand an agent everything on day one. You hand it the stuff that's costing you the most. These seven are the ones our customers keep saying "why didn't I do this months ago" about — because they're high-pain, low-effort, and the payoff shows up fast. Start here, in this order, and let the agent prove itself before you add a single thing more.

1. A weekly security scan and update pass

This is the chore nobody enjoys and everybody dreads. Log in, check for pending updates, hope nothing broke, run a scan, hold your breath. It's paranoid work — you do it over and over for the one time it actually saves you.

Hand it over. Tell the agent to run core and plugin updates on a schedule and to check your site against known vulnerabilities. It comes back in plain English: "Updated four plugins. One needed a closer look — here's what I found." You stop being the person who finds out there was a compromise six weeks late.

The reason to move this to the top of the list is simple. It's the one task where a single miss can cost you the whole site. A backup isn't a prevention. A scheduled check is.

2. Backups, confirmed not just scheduled

Most small site owners have backups ticking away somewhere and no idea if the last one actually worked. The plugin says "complete." Nobody verified it. Then the day you need it, it's corrupt, old, or was never running at all.

An agent changes that because it verifies, not just schedules. Ask it to check that backups are completing, that they're restorable, and to flag the moment one silently fails. It'll tell you "your last backup finished at 3am and restored clean on a test" — instead of leaving you to discover the gap on a bad Monday.

3. Site uptime and breakage watch

Your site being down is the worst problem you can have, because you usually find out from a customer, not from a dashboard. By the time someone emails you, you've already lost whatever they came to do.

Give this to the agent and it watches constantly. When the site dips, it checks what's causing it and sorts it out, then tells you what happened and what it fixed. You wake up to "the site sped up overnight after I cleared that cache, all good" instead of a frantic log-in at 2pm after a customer's message. The chores on this list all take pain off you — this one takes the fear off you.

4. Publishing on a schedule

You know the drill. You meant to post before the rush of the day, and then it was a client call, and then lunch, and then it's 6pm and the slot is gone. Content that publishes on a schedule moves your business forward on autopilot. Content that waits for you doesn't publish at all.

Hand the calendar to the agent. Tell it "post a tip every Tuesday at 9am," give it the topic or the outline, and it drafts and publishes on its own. When you give it a fuller idea — "write the monthly update, include last month's numbers" — it writes the post, formats it, and queues it for the slot you chose. Publishing becomes something that just happens, the same way a payroll person makes sure you get paid without you thinking about it.

5. Keeping the store shipping

If you run a WooCommerce store, you know the most draining part isn't the selling. It's the small stuff around it — the "did that order ship?" messages, the stock that quietly ran out, the prices that need changing for a sale. It's a dozen tiny decisions that each take a minute but thirty of them chew up an afternoon.

An agent handles the whole batch. Ask "how many orders today?" and get a number. Say "mark order #103 shipped" and it's done. "What am I about to run out of?" and it returns a list. It'll update stock when a supplier changes quantities, adjust a price across the store, and draft the banner for the next sale. Every one of these is a task you already know how to do — which is exactly why it should be handed off. You want your time on the parts only you can do.

6. Chasing and handling leads

The money leak in most small sites isn't traffic. It's the leads that arrive and then go quiet because nobody answered fast enough. You fill the form, someone gets an email that lands in spam, and the window closes. Speed is the whole game — a lead replied to in an hour converts far better than one answered tomorrow.

The agent watches your forms and comments. The moment someone raises their hand, you get a message — no dashboard log-in, no delay. And for the routine replies, the ones a human would answer with a template anyway, the agent handles them and keeps you in the loop. You stop losing sales to slow follow-up, which is a pay-off you feel in the ledger, not just in your workload.

7. A regular report you didn't have to build

Nobody wants to sit down and pull together "what happened on the site this month." You know it matters. You know the report is how you decide what's working. But it's the kind of admin that never makes it to the top of the list, because it's not urgent and it's fiddly.

Let the agent build it. Ask "what got the most visits this month?" or "send me a weekly summary of what happened on the site" and it gathers the analytics, the publishing, the store activity, the security events, and hands you one plain-English summary. Same insight you'd get from an hour of hunting through four dashboards, delivered to your chat. You get the clarity without the chore.

Why these seven, in this order

There's a logic to the list. Two of them are protection — the security scan and the backups — because you handout of fear. Two are about keeping the machinery running — uptime and publishing — because they quietly fail unless someone tends them. Two are where the money moves — the store and the leads — because they pay you back in revenue, not just time. And the report is the one that makes the rest of it legible, so you can see what the agent handled.

That's the point. You don't hire an agent for one nice-to-have task. You hire it for the seven things that stop costing you — in worry, in effort, in sales — as soon as they're off your plate.

How this works, without the setup

The agent connects to the WordPress site you already run, its store, forms and comments, and its analytics. Everything on this list happens from one chat that never clocks out. Give it these seven, let it prove the point, and the rest of your backlog starts to look like it'll take care of itself too.
