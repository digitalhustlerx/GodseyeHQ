Hire an AI agent instead of a developer (when you can)

Field notes from the Godseye team · 6 min read Guide

Here's the honest version of the headline: you shouldn't replace your developer with an AI agent. Most small WordPress owners shouldn't have a developer at all for the day-to-day, and that's a different sentence. The routine upkeep that's been arriving as separate $50 developer visits is exactly the kind of work that belongs on a flat plan. The big custom build you dream up next quarter still belongs to a human. This post is about telling the two apart.

Start with the money math

Run your numbers before you run for the phone. A small site's typical workload, priced the way most owners actually pay for it:

$50–$150per task for an agent visit — change an image, fix a page, sort a plugin conflict. Each one a fresh invoice.
$9 – $29 / montha flat agent plan that absorbs that same recurring work, flat, no per-job billing.

Do two or three upkeep jobs a month and you're spending $150–$450 on work that a flat plan covers for $9. The developer was never overcharging you — you were paying task rates for what is genuinely recurring, hourly-scale work. That's the mismatch.

The tasks that don't justify a developer visit

Anything you do more than once a month is recurring, and recurring work is where an agent earns its keep. If it's on this list, it's probably not worth a phone call:

  • Publishing a post or product "publish this week's article," "add this item with these specs." Drafted, formatted, and live, no-code.
  • Updating content a hero headline, a sale banner, a price change. Clicks, not code.
  • Routine maintenance core and plugin upkeep on a schedule, security scans, backups confirmed.
  • Image and link fixes a broken image, a dead link, missing alt text. Found and fixed in the same chat.
  • Orders and store admin "mark #103 shipped," check stock, flag what's running low.
  • Answers and reports "what happened on the site this month," "what got the most visits." Plain English, no dashboard login.

Try it once and you'll see the level. You tell your agent what you want in plain English over Telegram, it does the work, and you approve before anything touches the live site.

「 update the sale banner, publish this week's post, and tell me what I'm low on 」

Done. No ticket, no schedule slot, no surprise invoice.

Where a real developer still wins

This is where we stop being a sales pitch and get honest with you, because it protects us both. A paid professional developer is still the right call when:

  • It's a custom build. A bespoke theme, a complex plugin, a workflow that doesn't exist yet. That's a project, not a task, and it deserves someone who thinks in architecture.
  • It's a big launch. Rebuilding your store, migrating a thousand products, a log-in for hundreds of customers. That scale is a human job.
  • It's a real problem. A hacked site, a data migration, something that needs judgment and a deep look. An agent will flag it honestly; a developer fixes it.
  • It's one-off and finicky. A single job is fine to pay for once. The agent shines on the things that repeat.

An agent is a steady worker, not an architect. Plan that custom build with a developer and run the upkeep on the agent — that combo is what service businesses actually do, minus the retainer.

Think in "recurring vs. one-off"

The cleanest rule is one line: hire a developer for the thing that happens once, hire an agent for the thing that keeps happening. If the same visit has come around three or four times this year, it was never really a visit — it was a subscription pretending to be an invoice. Fold it into a flat plan.

Your developer keeps their job, your site stops racking up task fees, and 'the maintenance thing you keep meaning to schedule' just quietly gets done.
