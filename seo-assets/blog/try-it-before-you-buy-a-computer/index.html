Try it on your phone before you ever buy a computer

Field notes from the Godseye team · 3 min read Guide

Here's the thing nobody says out loud: you don't need a screen to get started with AI. Not a laptop, not a desktop, not a new tablet. If you've got a phone — and you do, you're reading this on one — you've got everything you need to start working with an AI business agent today.

Technology that meets you where you are

Most powerful software assumes you already have a computer. You have to buy the machine before you can use the tool, and the machine is the expensive part. That's a real barrier. A phone is the one device almost everyone already owns and already knows how to use.

An AI business agent runs in a chat on that phone. You text it the way you text your friends. There's no dashboard to open, no login to find, no layout to learn. The interface is the thing you already use all day. That doesn't just make it easier — it makes access to intelligence a lot more fair.

The computer becomes optional, not required

Think about it. Right now most of us use a computer because we have no choice — it's the only way to get certain work done. With an agent you can text, that changes. The computer is still there if you want it, but it's no longer the gatekeeper.

  • Run your business from your phone. Check sales, approve a post, answer a customer — all from a message.
  • Get real work done, not just answers. An agent publishes, updates, and fixes things. It does work.
  • Keep the computer for when you want it. Big project? Kick off work from your phone, finish on a screen if you feel like it. Your call, not the software's.

Try before you buy the machine

Here's the part that makes this worth trying right now: you can test the whole thing economically before you ever spend money on a computer. Most of that machine's job — organizing, communicating, executing — an agent can start doing from your phone for a few dollars. If it works for you, great. If you decide you still want a full computer, you'll have had a chance to find out first, instead of gambling on a big purchase blind.

That's a low-risk way in. Start small, see if the work gets done, and grow from there when it pays off.

Accessible isn't just a nice-to-have

Making powerful tools work from a phone, and letting them pay for themselves before you upgrade your setup, is what "accessible" actually means. It's not a slogan. It means someone who doesn't own a computer yet can still hire an AI teammate today, get real work done, and decide for themselves what to buy next. The capability no longer sits behind an expensive screen.
